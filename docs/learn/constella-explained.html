The one line

You tell Constella what your money is for, in plain words. It builds a basket of real-world assets on Stellar to match, locks your rules into code, and gives you one token you can hold, cash out any time, or borrow against.

The founding document says it more tightly: Constella turns a plain-English thesis into a rule-bound basket of Stellar's tokenized real-world assets, packaged as one non-custodial token that ships collateral-ready.

Notice what's missing: "AI portfolio manager." The AI is one part. It drafts. It never holds money, never trades, and never decides anything on its own. The product is the basket token with your rules locked in, and the fact that lenders can accept it as collateral.

Why it exists

The problem: billions of dollars sitting still

Big financial firms have started putting traditional investments on blockchains. Franklin Templeton's government money fund (BENJI) alone holds over $1.1B on chain, and it lives on Stellar. In total Stellar holds more than $4.1B of these "tokenized real-world assets."

But only about $198M of value is actually being used in Stellar's lending and trading apps. Over 95% just sits in wallets. The Stellar Development Foundation's framing for this is useful → usable → used: assets exist (useful), but they're hard to combine and put to work (not yet usable), so almost nobody does (not used).

Why they sit still

  • Each one is a separate island. A company that wants "safe dollars, some euros, some yield" has to research, buy and watch five different tokens from five issuers.
  • Rules live in someone's head. "Never more than 30% with one issuer" is a policy in a spreadsheet, not something the system enforces.
  • Lenders won't accept them. A lender can't easily price a mixed bag of tokens, doesn't know how fast it could be sold, and may not be allowed to hold some of them. So you can't borrow against your holdings.

Constella fixes all three: one basket, rules enforced by code, and a token designed so lenders can accept it.

The minimum finance you need

Finance from zero

Real-world asset (RWA) and tokenization

A "real-world asset" is something that exists off the blockchain: a US government bond, a money market fund, a euro. Tokenizing it means an issuer holds the real thing and issues a digital token that represents it, one token per unit. The token can move instantly, 24/7, on a blockchain.

The assets in our world

  • Stablecoins (USDC, EURC): tokens worth exactly 1 dollar or 1 euro, issued by Circle and backed by cash in banks. Think "digital cash." Instant, but earn nothing on their own.
  • T-bills: short-term loans to the US government (or a European one). The safest common investment there is. They pay a small interest rate.
  • Money market / T-bill funds (BENJI, Spiko, USDY): funds that hold T-bills and pass the interest to you. On chain, you hold a token; its value slowly grows. Cashing out can take up to a day.
  • Lending (Blend): you lend your USDC into a pool; borrowers pay interest; you earn part of it. You get "bTokens" that represent your share of the pool.
  • Tokenized equities (later): stocks or index trackers on chain, like JQQQ (a Nasdaq-100 tracker), for professional investors.

Permissionless vs gated

USDC, EURC and Blend are permissionless: anyone can hold them. BENJI, USDY, Spiko and JQQQ are gated: the issuer must approve each holder (identity checks, sometimes only professional investors, sometimes not in certain countries). That's why our testnet baskets use only USDC, EURC and Blend today, and why the gated ones are shown in the app as "coming with issuer partners."

Liquidity: how fast can you get cash

Liquidity means "how quickly can I turn this into cash without losing value." USDC is instant. A T-bill fund might take a day. Some assets take a week. A basket mixes them, so we always keep a slice in instant cash. That slice is called the liquidity floor.

The core idea

The product: a basket, its rules, and one token

Basket and sleeves

A basket is a mix of assets held together. Each asset's slot in the basket is a sleeve ("the USDC sleeve," "the Blend sleeve"). Each sleeve has a target weight, its share of the basket.

Real example · testnet basket CDAJDSXB, "18 months of runway, a third in euro"
US dollar cash (USDC)target 50.8%
Euro cash (EURC)target 34.2%
USDC lending on Blendtarget 15.0%

Bands: how far a sleeve may drift

Prices move, so weights drift. Each sleeve has a band, an allowed range around its target. In that basket the band is ±2 percentage points, so euro may sit anywhere from 32.2% to 36.2%. Inside the band, nothing happens. Outside it, the keeper rebalances.

The vault and its token (shares)

The basket lives inside a vault: a smart contract on Stellar that holds the assets. When you put money in, the vault gives you shares: tokens that represent your slice of everything inside. That share token is "the one token." You can hold it, send it, redeem it for cash, and (soon) borrow against it.

The vault currency

Every vault keeps its books in one currency, its numéraire (we say "vault currency"). Ours is USDC: you deposit USDC, values are shown in dollars, redemptions pay USDC. Euro-currency vaults need a euro price feed on chain, which we don't have yet.

Shared basket vs your own basket

There's one shared basket anyone can join (the Cash Reserve: 85% USDC, 15% Blend lending). And anyone can create their own private basket from their thesis, with one signature. That's the core flow.

AI proposes, contracts dispose

What the AI actually does

You type a thesis, for example "Keep 18 months of runway safe, hold a third in euro, at least 10% instant cash." Our engine turns it into a basket in six stages:

1ParseAn AI model reads your words and fills a strict form: currencies, cash floor, time horizon, caps. Rules double-check it.
2RetrieveFinds only assets in our registry that you are eligible to hold in your country. It can't invent assets.
3OptimiseMath, not AI, picks weights that meet every constraint. If impossible, it says why and shows the closest basket.
4SimulateStress tests: worst exit time, a stablecoin losing its peg, an issuer freezing. Shown before you sign.
5ExplainA diff: what you asked for vs what you'd get, with the reason for each difference.
6SignOnly you sign. The rules get a fingerprint (policy hash) and are locked on chain.

The principle from the spec: the AI proposes; the contracts dispose. The AI's output is only a draft. Nothing reaches the chain until you review and sign, and the contracts check every trade against your signed rules, not against anything the AI says.

Why "closed universe" matters

AI models sometimes make things up. If ours suggested a token that doesn't exist or that you can't legally hold, that would be dangerous. So the engine can only pick from a curated list (the registry), filtered by your country and investor type. Ask for "50% Tesla stock" and it tells you that's outside the universe.

What "rule-bound" means

Rules and safety

When you sign, these rules are stored in a policy contract and enforced on every trade. Here are the real rules from the euro runway basket:

Signed policy · CDAJDSXB
Instant cash at least (liquidity floor)10%
Any single asset at most40%
Any single issuer at most40%
Rebalance when a sleeve drifts by2 points
Maximum slippage per trade0.5%
  • Issuer cap: USDC and EURC are both issued by Circle, so together they count as one issuer. This stops "all eggs with one company."
  • Slippage: the difference between the fair price and the price you actually get when trading. The rule says: never accept more than 0.5% worse than fair. If a trade would be worse, it is refused.
  • Policy hash: a fingerprint of the exact rules. Anyone can check that the rules on chain are the ones you signed. Changing them needs your signature again; loosening them waits 48 hours with notice.
  • Post-trade check: after every rebalance, the vault re-measures what it actually holds. If any rule is broken, the whole trade is undone.

Non-custodial

"Non-custodial" means Constella never holds your money. The vault contract holds it, and only you can redeem your shares. Our admin can pause a vault or propose a code upgrade, but upgrades wait 48 hours in public first, and no admin key can move user funds.

How money moves

"Value per share" is the NAV per share (net asset value: everything in the vault, divided by the shares). The app shows the conservative NAV: when two price sources disagree, we use the lower one, so nobody gets overpaid.

The keeper and rebalancing

The keeper is our automated bot. Every 5 minutes it checks every vault. If a sleeve is outside its band, it proposes trades to fix it. The vault only accepts trades that pass your rules. The keeper can't take money out; it can only swap between the basket's own assets inside the vault.

Flow netting: trade as little as possible

Every trade costs a fee and some slippage, so we avoid them. Deposits and withdrawals land in cash first and cancel each other out. When a rebalance is needed, new cash buys the most underweight sleeves (never selling anything), and cash needed for withdrawals comes from the most overweight sleeves. Sleeves that are close to target are left alone.

Where trades happen: AMMs and pools

To swap USDC for EURC, the keeper uses Soroswap, an AMM (automated market maker). An AMM is a pool holding two tokens; the price comes from the ratio between them. A big trade in a small pool moves the price a lot. That's why euro baskets are stuck on testnet:

Real numbers · testnet USDC/EURC pool, 26 Nov
Pool holds~263 USDC / ~240 EURC
A $100 euro basket needs to buy ~34 USDC of EURCmoves the price ~13%
The basket's rule allows0.5% slippage
So the keeper refuses and keeps that part in cashcorrect behaviour

On mainnet the real euro markets are thousands of times deeper, so this is a testnet limitation, not a product one.

Oracles: where prices come from

A contract can't look up a price on the internet. An oracle is a contract that publishes prices on chain. We use two independent sources for EURC (Reflector and our own feed from the European Central Bank rate) and take the safer one. If prices go stale or disagree too much, a circuit breaker freezes that price.

Frozen prices and claims

If an asset can't be priced (its oracle froze), a normal redemption can't value your shares. So there's a special exit: you get the cash part now, plus a claim for the frozen part. When the price returns, the keeper sells that part and pays your claim in USDC. You're never locked out completely.

Stress tests you see before signing

  • Redemption ladder: how much of the basket you could get out instantly, within 24 hours, within a week.
  • Depeg: what happens if a stablecoin stops being worth exactly $1.
  • Issuer freeze: what happens if one issuer halts redemptions.
  • Worst-case exit time: the longest it could take to get everything out.

Every simulation carries this exact sentence, a legal requirement in our rules: "This simulation uses historical data. Past performance does not predict future returns. This is not financial advice."

The headline feature

The Collateral Passport

Collateral is something you pledge so you can borrow. A lender lets you borrow USDC against your basket token; if you don't pay back, they can sell the token. This lets a company spend without selling its investments.

Lenders normally refuse mixed baskets. The Passport is the set of features that makes our token acceptable:

  1. Conservative price: one price feed for the token (in the SEP-40 standard every Stellar lender reads), always the lowest valid price. If it can't price the token, lending stops.
  2. Exit capacity: how much could be cashed out right now, in 24 hours, in a week. Lenders cap loans by it.
  3. Risk sheet: a recommended LTV (loan-to-value: borrow at most this share of the collateral's value, for example 80%).
  4. Compliant liquidation: if a loan must be closed, only approved parties can buy the gated assets.

The number we'll be judged on: what share of all money in Constella is posted as collateral. That's the measure of "used."

Testnet today: the price feed for vault shares works and the Cash Reserve is listed. Borrowing is off until our Blend lending pool's backstop is funded, which needs BLND tokens from the Blend team.

No seed phrase, no gas

Accounts and getting in

  • Passkey account: sign in with Face ID, Touch ID or Windows Hello. No seed phrase to lose. Under the hood it's a smart account, a contract that checks your passkey's signature.
  • Zero gas: every blockchain action costs a tiny network fee in XLM. Our relayer pays it for passkey users, through a fee sponsor contract that only pays for allowed actions.
  • Stellar wallet: people who already use Freighter, xBull, LOBSTR and similar can connect instead. They sign in their wallet and pay their own fees.
  • Devices: add your phone to the same account. Both screens show the same 6-character check code, and your first device approves.
  • Guardians and recovery: if you lose every passkey, guardians you chose (your other devices or trusted people) approve a new passkey. It takes over only after 48 hours, and you can cancel meanwhile. Guardians can never move money.
Revenue

How Constella earns

Decided model (D-D, enforced in the vault contract): a performance fee of 10% of the profit, and only above the high-water mark (the best value per share reached so far; you never pay twice for the same gains). There is an all-in ceiling of 0.20% of assets per year. No profit, no fee. No deposit or redemption fees.

How to say it

10 seconds

Tell us what your money is for; we turn it into a basket of real-world assets on Stellar, with your rules locked into code, as one token you can hold, cash out or borrow against.

30 seconds

Stellar has over four billion dollars of tokenized treasuries and stablecoins, and 95% of it sits idle. Constella lets a company write its treasury plan in plain English, say "18 months of runway, a third in euro, 10% always in cash." Our engine builds a matching basket from assets the company can legally hold, shows the stress tests, and when they sign, the rules are enforced on chain. They get one token they can redeem any time or use as collateral to borrow.

2 minutes

Add, in order: who it's for (startups, DAOs and funds holding stablecoins), why lenders accept it (the Collateral Passport: conservative pricing, exit capacity, compliant liquidation), why it's safe (non-custodial, the AI only drafts, contracts check every trade, 48-hour timelocks), and how we earn (10% of profit, never more than 0.20% a year, nothing if there's no profit).

Phrases to avoid

  • "AI portfolio manager" or "AI treasury manager": the AI drafts; it doesn't manage.
  • "Guaranteed," "safe," "risk-free," or any yield number: nothing is guaranteed.
  • "Audited": not yet; the audit comes before mainnet.
  • "Your keys never leave your device": passkeys can sync through iCloud or Google.

Hard questions, short answers

Is this a fund?

No. Each user's basket is their own vault with their own rules; Constella provides software. Gated assets and professional clients come through licensed partners.

Who holds the money?

The vault contract. Only the share owner can redeem. Constella's admin can pause, but can't move user funds.

What if the AI gets it wrong?

The AI only fills a draft. Math builds the basket, you review the diff and the stress tests, and you sign. After that, contracts check every trade against the signed rules, not against the AI.

Why Stellar?

That's where the real-world assets are (BENJI, USDC, EURC, Spiko), fees are tiny, and Stellar's new features (passkeys, fee sponsorship, Bell's W-addresses) make it usable for non-crypto people.

How is this different from Yearn or Morpho vaults?

Those optimise yield on crypto assets with rules set by a curator. Constella starts from your own plain-English goals, uses real-world assets you're eligible for, enforces your rules on chain, and is built to be borrowed against.

What happens in a crisis?

Prices freeze when sources disagree, trades that break the rules are refused, a slice is always instant cash, and if an asset is frozen you still get the cash part out with a claim for the rest.
